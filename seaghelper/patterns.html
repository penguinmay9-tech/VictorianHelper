Research guide · Primary 7

SEAG question patterns
2023–2025

A year-by-year research guide to SEAG English and Maths. It explains what the official documents confirm, what can be seen in released practice papers, and which curriculum skills are worth revising.

Important: SEAG does not publish an official list saying that a particular misspelt word or exact maths topic will appear in a particular year. This page separates confirmed paper format from useful revision topics. These are patterns to prepare for, not predictions or guaranteed questions.

What to practise in English

Spelling

There are 5 multiple-choice spelling questions in each paper. Practise recognising correct UK spelling and spotting a misspelt word in a sentence.

Commonly confused wordsHomophonesDouble lettersPrefixes and suffixesVowel patternsSilent letters

Examples to revise: separate, necessary, beginning, believe, favourite, accidentally. These are practice examples, not a claimed list from a specific year's paper.

Grammar

There are 5 multiple-choice grammar questions. Revise word classes and how words fit together in a sentence.

Punctuation

There are 5 multiple-choice punctuation questions. Practise checking a whole sentence and identifying the part that needs correcting.

What to practise in Maths

SEAG lists six broad Maths areas: Numbers, Measurements, Shape and Space, Money, Probability and Data Representation. The skills below are examples within those areas, not a claim that every topic appeared in every year.

Number

  • Addition, subtraction, multiplication and division
  • Fractions, decimals and percentages
  • Factors, multiples and number patterns
  • Rounding, place value and negative numbers
  • Ratio and proportion

Measure and shape

  • Length, mass, capacity and unit conversions
  • Time and timetables
  • Perimeter, area and volume
  • Angles, 2D shapes and 3D shapes
  • Scale and position

Money and data

  • Money calculations and change
  • Tables, charts and graphs
  • Averages and range
  • Interpreting information

Reasoning

  • Multi-step word problems
  • Choosing the correct operation
  • Explaining or showing a method
  • Probability and comparing possibilities

How the assessment is structured

SectionQuestionsAnswer style
English: punctuation → grammar → spelling15 total (5 each)Multiple choice
Comprehension137 multiple choice + 6 written
Maths2822 multiple choice + 6 written
Main assessment5660 minutes

Each sitting includes two papers. Each paper has its own 60-minute main test of 56 questions, plus an untimed, unmarked warm-up of 5 English and 5 Maths questions. Pupils may start the main test with English or Maths.
