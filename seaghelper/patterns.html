Research guide · Primary 7

SEAG question patterns
2023–2025

A year-by-year research guide to SEAG English and Maths. It explains what the official documents confirm, what can be seen in released practice papers, and which curriculum skills are worth revising.

Important: The official public documents confirm the paper format and broad curriculum, but they do not provide a verified list of every exact live question from 2023 and 2024. The word examples below are revision examples, not claimed past-paper answers. For 2025, SEAG says official Practice Papers 3 and 4 contain well over 90% of questions from the actual assessment.

Common revision lists found in public resources

I checked public SEAG information and publicly indexed tutor/revision posts. I could not verify separate Facebook lists of exact words or topics for 2023, 2024 and 2025. The lists below are useful revision areas, not claims about questions that definitely appeared in a particular year.

What the year tabs mean: 2023 and 2024 used the same assessment specification and format. For 2025, SEAG later released Practice Papers 3 and 4 containing well over 90% of questions from the 2025 assessment. That makes the official 2025 papers the best source for genuine examples.

Homophones

These are independent practice examples, not a recovered list from the 2023, 2024 or 2025 live papers.

Grammar — topics to practise

The official specification confirms these areas:

  • Nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs
  • Conjunctions and prepositions
  • Present, past, future and conditional verb forms
  • Irregular verbs, such as seek/sought and find/found
  • Contractions, such as they’re and could’ve
  • Plurals, including irregular and collective nouns
  • Comparative and superlative adjectives/adverbs
  • Phrases, sentences, paragraphs and chapters

Punctuation — topics to practise

The official specification names these punctuation marks:

  • Capital letters and full stops
  • Commas
  • Colons and semi-colons
  • Brackets and hyphens
  • Apostrophes
  • Question marks and exclamation marks
  • Speech marks and quotation marks
  • Proofreading short sentences and passages to spot errors

Maths — topics to practise

The official specification gives the broad areas and examples below:

  • Number: place value, four operations, decimals, fractions, percentages, rounding, sequences, prime/square/cube numbers and simple unknowns
  • Measurement: length, mass, capacity, time, temperature, unit conversions, perimeter, area, volume and scale
  • Shape and space: 2D/3D shapes, angles, symmetry, coordinates, compass directions and turns
  • Money: calculations, estimation and change
  • Probability: likely, unlikely, certain, impossible and simple probability
  • Data: tables, tally charts, bar/line/pie charts, Venn diagrams, mean and range

How the assessment is structured

SectionQuestionsAnswer style
English: punctuation → grammar → spelling15 total (5 each)Multiple choice
Comprehension137 multiple choice + 6 written
Maths2822 multiple choice + 6 written
Main assessment5660 minutes

Each sitting includes two papers. Each paper has its own 60-minute main test of 56 questions, plus an untimed, unmarked warm-up of 5 English and 5 Maths questions. Pupils may start the main test with English or Maths.
